# NFL backtest — 2024 + 2025, no real prices (NFLBT-2026-09-15)

Owner, after week 1 (−12.05u): *"can we do a backtest like we did with baseball?"* → *"just do it
without the odds"*.

## Why there are no real prices, and what stands in for them

nflverse has every touchdown back to 1999 and **no anytime-TD prices at all**. The 2026-08-24 cold
backtest showed the trap: price the field off our own inputs and any selector prints a fake edge
(+18% that collapsed to −4.8%). So a price here is a **model of the market at a stated sharpness**,
and every rule is run against four of them. The answer is read *across* the four.

| book | what the book knows | AUC 2025 (played rows) |
|---|---|---|
| `weak` | less than us (our model + noise) — the only world where we have an edge | 0.720 |
| `same` | exactly what we know (our model, honestly calibrated) | 0.766 |
| `rich` | ours + prior TD rate, sample size, game line | 0.770 |
| `sharp` | rich, pulled 10% toward same-game usage | 0.804 |
| *our model* | | *0.766* |

Real week 1 (244 priced, 57 scorers): market AUC 0.72 vs ours 0.73 — too few to separate, which is
why `same`/`rich`/`sharp` are all treated as plausible and `weak` is shown but not relied on.

Mechanics, all in this folder:
- **Offered field**: top 8 per team by book probability among players who played (≈ the real 7.6).
- **Calibration**: each book is recalibrated leave-one-season-out on its own offered rows with a
  smooth (cubic-in-logit) map. A linear logistic left the +150..+500 band ~2pp under-priced —
  zero vig for anything living there. Isotonic was tried and rejected: its step function gives
  dozens of players one price and our model then picks inside the tie with information the book
  threw away.
- **Margin**: probability × 1.15, rounded like a book. Flat-betting every offered price returns
  −4% to −13% by book/season, bracketing week 1's real ≈ −10%.
- **Engine**: the LIVE scorer (`nfl_mock.score`, fed the week's table) and the LIVE draft
  (`nfl_draft_cli.js` with only `MAX_ODDS` made settable), graded by `soccer_grade.grade_ticket`.
  One slate per game day, like production.
- **Leakage**: `nfl_model.json` was fit on 2021–24, so **2024 flatters our model**; 2025 is clean.
  Depth charts are read as of Sept 1 (pre-season).

## Results — realistic worlds (`same` + `rich` + `sharp`), 2024 + 2025 pooled

115 slates (58 in 2024, 57 in 2025) × 3 books per rule. 90% intervals are a week-level bootstrap.

If the market is **sharper** than us (`sharp` alone): no cap −73%, +800 −46%, +500 −36%,
+400 −34%, +300 −15%, market-only +5%. If it is **level** with us (`same` + `rich`): +500 +12%,
+400 +17%, no cap −9%. Full table per season × book in `summary.csv`.

## What it says

1. **The longshot tilt was the damage, and it was structural.** Uncapped EV ranking is the worst
   rule in every realistic world, and catastrophic (−73%) whenever the market knows more than we
   do. Week 1's result is what this rule should be expected to do, not just a bad draw.
2. **The price ceiling is the fix that matters.** No cap → +800 → +500 improves at every step.
   +400 and +500 are within noise of each other; +400 comes out slightly ahead.
3. **The model is not shown to beat the price.** Market-only ranking inside the band does as well
   as any model weighting. What the band does is stop the board from betting on its own errors
   at the long end. Treat the model as a tie-breaker, not an edge.
4. **Nothing here is proven profitable.** Every capped rule's interval spans zero.
5. **Moons are fine once capped** (≈ break-even at +500/+400), and bad without a cap.
